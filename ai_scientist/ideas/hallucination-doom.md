#Title Hallucination Nation: Understanding and Mitigating Misinformation in Generative AI

##Keywords generative AI, hallucination, misinformation, LLMs, trustworthiness, evaluation

##TL;DR Why do generative AI models often "make things up"? This research explores the phenomenon of hallucination in large language models, investigating its root causes, manifestations across modalities, and strategies for reliable mitigation.

##Abstract
With the rapid deployment of generative AI models—especially large language models (LLMs)—into real-world applications, a pressing challenge has emerged: hallucination. These models frequently produce outputs that are plausible but factually incorrect or nonsensical, undermining their trustworthiness in critical domains such as healthcare, law, education, and scientific research. The “Hallucination Nation” research theme seeks to deeply investigate this phenomenon across different architectures, modalities (text, image, multimodal), and use cases. We aim to answer key questions such as: What are the core architectural or training-related reasons behind hallucination? How do task design and prompt structure influence hallucination rates? Can we develop a principled framework to evaluate hallucination severity and impact? And what mitigation strategies—ranging from fine-tuning and retrieval augmentation to chain-of-thought prompting—are most effective in minimizing misinformation? This research will draw from diverse fields including machine learning, cognitive science, HCI, and ethics to build a multidisciplinary understanding of hallucination. By providing empirical insights and proposing robust evaluation protocols, we hope to guide the development of more reliable and trustworthy generative systems. Ultimately, tackling hallucination head-on is essential not only for technical progress, but also for the safe and responsible integration of AI into society.
